blurb: >-
  Paris, 1824. A small rug trading business on the Rue Saint-Germain became the
  foundation of Braquenié, later joined by the Braquenié brothers to create one
  of France’s most celebrated textile houses. By the 1850s, 16 Rue Vivienne had
  become a destination for European aristocracy, with commissions from Napoleon
  III, the Kings of Belgium and Spain, the Vatican and Victor Hugo. In 1875,
  King Leopold II of Belgium granted the house the title of Manufacture Royale.
---

Today, under the custodianship of Pierre Frey, Braquenié’s extraordinary archive
continues through fabrics, wallcoverings and hand-woven rugs inspired by the
grandeur of the Second Empire, the elegance of Art Nouveau and the geometry of
Art Deco. A house of French textile heritage and enduring panache, shaped by two
centuries of craftsmanship and refinement.
