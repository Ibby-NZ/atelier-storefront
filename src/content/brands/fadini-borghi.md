  Turin, 1947. Osvaldo Fadini founded an upholstery house that would come to
  specialise in a single material, silk, worked in the great Italian tradition
  that stretched back to the Renaissance explorers who first carried the craft
  home from the Orient. His nephew Paolo took the reins at just twenty-five and
  in 1970 joined forces with Borghi, the legendary house behind Genoa’s
  incomparable cut velvets, uniting two of Italy’s most storied silk traditions
  under one name.
---

The result is fabric of real theatrical grandeur: ciselé velvets, taffetas,
damasks, lampas and brocatelles, reinterpreted from sixteenth-century Italian
classics, their patterns as at home in La Scala or on the Belle Époque walls of
Monte Carlo as in a private salon.

Now part of the Pierre Frey stable, Fadini Borghi remains the house silk purists
turn to; opulent, historically fluent, and utterly, unmistakably Italian.
