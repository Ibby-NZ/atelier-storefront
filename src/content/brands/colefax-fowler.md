  In 1930s London, Sibyl Colefax, a society hostess who had lost much of her
  fortune in the Wall Street Crash, transformed her instinctive eye for
  interiors into a decorating business. In 1938, she brought in rising decorator
  John Fowler, whose mastery of colour and proportion gave the house its
  defining aesthetic. A few years later, Nancy Lancaster from the American South
  acquired the firm, introducing her effortless approach to rooms that felt
  collected rather than decorated.
---

Together, Lancaster and Fowler shaped what became known as English Country House
style; faded chintz beside period silk, humble pieces alongside the grand and
interiors designed for living.

That heritage continues across Colefax & Fowler fabrics, wallcoverings and
trimmings. Drawing from historic archives, the collections feature trailing
florals, soft jacquards, elegant stripes and muted, weathered palettes inspired
by the passage of time. The result is quintessentially English decoration;
refined yet relaxed, timeless yet welcoming, with a sense of comfort and
understated sophistication.
