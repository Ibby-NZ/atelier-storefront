  Paris, 1928. Félix Houlès and his son André set up shop at 18 Rue
  Saint-Nicolas, in the Faubourg Saint-Antoine — the historic heart of Paris’s
  upholsterers, cabinetmakers and decorators. What began as a small supplier of
  upholstery essentials grew, through Suzanne and Pierre Houlès, into a genuine
  art form: passementerie, the centuries-old French craft of decorative
  trimmings first perfected for European royalty between the 17th and 19th
  centuries.
---

Four generations on, now led by great-grandson Philippe Houlès, the maison has
dressed the Château de Versailles, the Bolshoi Theatre and presidential
residences worldwide with its tassels, fringes, cords, braids and tiebacks —
each piece revisiting a century-deep archive with real precision and restraint.

Alongside its trimmings, Houlès has built an equally exceptional offering in
curtain hardware: poles, finials and fittings engineered with the same
jeweller’s precision as the passementerie itself, right through to the Tantulo
collection’s fully realised decorative systems. This is the finishing touch
elevated to genuine craft — intricate, architectural, and unmistakably Parisian
in spirit, from the smallest tassel to the rod it hangs from.
